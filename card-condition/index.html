Card Condition Guide

A fast, practical way to evaluate raw Pokémon cards using TCGplayer's published conditioning framework. This is about assigning a marketplace condition—not predicting a PSA grade.

Based on TCGplayer Card Conditioning Standards · March 2025 ↗
Professor Palm's rule: Condition conservatively. If a card sits on the border between two conditions, use the lower one. A pleasant surprise for the buyer is cheaper than a return, dispute, or damaged reputation.

The 30-second inspection

1 · Light

Remove the sleeve and inspect the front and back under bright, direct light. Tilt the card to expose scratches, scuffing, dents, and surface wear.

2 · Edges

Check all four edges and corners for whitening, nicks, flaking, fraying, dents, or material loss.

3 · Surface

Look for scratches, grime, bends, creases, indentations, peeling, liquid exposure, and printing defects.

4 · Assign

Identify the worst imperfection types, estimate severity, add the TCGplayer point values, then apply any condition-specific exclusions.

Condition at a glance

TCGplayer uses five marketplace conditions and does not list cards as Mint or Gem Mint. Imperfection severity is scored as Slight = 1, Minor = 2, Moderate = 4, and Major = 8 points.

NM
Up to 3 points

Minimal handling or play wear. No surface wear, grime, bends, faults, or damage.

LP
Up to 6 points

Visible handling/play signs without significant imperfections. Faults and damage are not allowed.

MP
Up to 12 points

Significant play, use, or wear. Minor faults can appear, but true damage is not allowed.

HP
Up to 24 points

Heavy, obvious wear across several areas. It can still be structurally usable, but damage is not allowed.

DMG
Beyond other standards

Tears, punctures, splitting, folding, major liquid-related faults, integrity problems, or other severe issues.

What each condition can tolerate

ConditionExamples that can fitAutomatic problems
Near MintSlight edgewear, slight scuffing, slight indentation, minor scratches, slight manufacturing defect—while staying within 3 total points.Not NM: surface wear, grime, bend, fault, or damage.
Lightly PlayedMinor edgewear/scratches/scuffing/indentation/bend; slight surface wear or grime; minor defect—within 6 points.Not LP: fault or damage.
Moderately PlayedModerate edgewear/scratches/scuffing/indentation; minor surface wear, bends, grime, faults, or defects—within 12 points.Not MP: damage.
Heavily PlayedMajor edgewear/scratches/scuffing/grime; moderate surface wear, indentations, bends, or defects; minor faults—within 24 points.Not HP: damage/integrity failure.
DamagedUse when the card exceeds the other standards or has severe integrity-affecting issues such as tears, punctures, splitting, folding, serious liquid exposure, missing/illegible information, or severe shifting/miscuts.

The imperfection vocabulary

EdgewearLoss of color, sheen, or material along edges, corners, or borders.
ScratchA scored line on the surface that removes or marks material.
ScuffingA cluster of small scratches or abrasions; clouding and sleeve/binder gloss can fall here.
Surface wearMaterial loss that exposes lighter/internal layers through the printed image or color.
GrimeMaterial left on the card through handling, play, or poor storage.
IndentationA dent, ding, groove, impression, or pockmark that displaces material.
BendA line or ridge created by folding or pressing; includes creases.
FaultSplitting or expansion of the surface/material, including rippling, peeling, delamination, fraying, or liquid-related effects.
DamageA severe imperfection that affects card integrity: tears, holes, flimsiness, sun bleaching, foreign substances, peels, and similar issues.
DefectA printing/manufacturing issue such as crimping, miscutting, shifted printing, roller marks, foil bubbling, or ink problems.

Two easy-to-miss TCGplayer rules

Centering: TCGplayer allows up to roughly 70/30 centering without changing condition. More severe shifting is handled as a photo listing rather than simply knocking the card down a condition. Curling: up to 5 mm of flex can be tolerated without changing condition; beyond that threshold TCGplayer treats the card as Damaged.

How I would use this when buying collections

What you seeBuying posture
Looks NM at first glanceStill inspect the back, corners, and holo surface. A single crease, dent, or hidden surface issue can materially change the condition.
Borderline NM / LPValue it as LP unless you have inspected it closely under direct light. Do not pay NM money for uncertainty.
Obvious MPOnly buy if the discount and liquidity justify the extra selling friction. For inventory consolidation, this usually should not be your target.
HP / DamagedDefault pass for normal inventory unless it is a very liquid/high-value card bought at a steep enough discount with a clear exit.
