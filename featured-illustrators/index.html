Featured Illustrators

Six Pokémon TCG artists I actively spotlight in my store. Each profile gives you a fast read on the artist’s visual signature, a representative card, and direct access to cards I currently have listed.

Yuka Morii · Shibuzoh. · Asako Ito · Tomokazu Komiya · Shinji Kanda · sowsow
Professor’s note: collecting by illustrator is one of the best ways to make low-cost cards feel intentional. These pages focus on recognizable styles rather than price alone.
Clay sculpture photography

Yuka Morii

Morii sculpts Pokémon in clay and photographs the models in built or real-world environments. The tactile texture makes her cards immediately identifiable even before you read the illustrator line.

Representative card

Pumpkaboo — Evolving Skies
Classic Morii: a tiny sculpted subject photographed outdoors with real texture and light.

Soft, playful character art

Shibuzoh.

Shibuzoh.’s work leans rounded, colorful and character-forward. Pokémon often sit inside friendly pastel worlds that feel closer to picture-book illustration than dramatic battle art.

Representative card

Claydol — Battle Styles
A usually eerie Pokémon rendered inside a soft rainbow landscape—an excellent snapshot of Shibuzoh.’s tone.

Crochet / amigurumi photography

Asako Ito

Ito crochets amigurumi Pokémon, builds tactile backgrounds from materials such as felt, and photographs the finished scene. The handmade construction is the art—not a digital simulation of yarn.

Representative card

Altaria — Crown Zenith GG
A fully crafted domestic scene built around a crocheted Altaria, showing how far Ito can push the handmade format.

Primitivist, distorted, expressive

Tomokazu Komiya

A veteran TCG illustrator known for deliberately distorted proportions, loose color and expressive compositions. Komiya has described leaning into the strange, unmistakable quality people expect from his work.

Representative card

Drowzee — Scarlet & Violet IR
Dense, warped perspective and expressive color make it a modern showcase of Komiya’s instantly recognizable visual language.

Surreal, dense, high-energy

Shinji Kanda

Kanda’s newer TCG work is often packed with layered detail, unusual textures and dramatic motion. Pokémon frequently feel embedded in a full environment rather than simply posed in front of one.

Representative card

Magikarp — Paldea Evolved IR
A maximalist waterfall scene full of hidden detail and texture—the card that introduced many collectors to Kanda’s style.

Warm, painterly everyday scenes

sowsow

sowsow builds soft, lived-in scenes with a story-book sensibility. The work extends beyond the TCG into Pokémon Sleep, Poké Lid art, and the 2023 Pokémon × Van Gogh collaboration.

Representative card

Gengar VMAX — Fusion Strike
A major collector favorite that turns Gengar into an entire surreal landscape while keeping the palette playful rather than grim.
